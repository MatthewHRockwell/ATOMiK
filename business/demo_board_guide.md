## What You're Holding

This is a **Sipeed Tang Nano 9K** — a $13.50 FPGA development board running the ATOMiK delta-state computing architecture. It contains a custom RISC-V processor (RV64I) with ATOMiK hardware instructions built directly into the silicon fabric, outputting to a 1280x720 HDMI display.

**This is not a simulation.** Every number on screen is computed live by the hardware in your hand.

## Quick Start

1. **Connect HDMI** to any monitor or TV (standard HDMI cable)
2. **Connect USB-C** to any USB power source (charger, laptop, battery pack)
3. The board boots automatically and begins cycling through 7 dashboard screens

No keyboard, mouse, or software required. The demo runs in an infinite loop.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Is this a pre-recorded demo?**
A: No. Every number is computed live by the hardware on each loop iteration. The random matrix changes each cycle. The benchmark timings are measured in real-time. If you power-cycle the board, the self-test runs again from scratch.

**Q: Why does it say "Provisionally Patented"?**
A: A provisional patent application has been filed covering the delta-state XOR accumulation architecture, parallel bank design, binary merge tree, and schema-driven RTL generation. The provisional establishes priority date; a full utility patent filing is planned as part of post-funding IP prosecution.

**Q: What does "108 theorems, 0 sorry" mean?**
A: The mathematical properties of ATOMiK's delta-state algebra are formally verified using Lean4, a proof assistant used in cutting-edge mathematics. "0 sorry" means zero unproven assumptions — every theorem is fully proven from axioms. This is the highest standard of correctness in computer science.

**Q: How does this scale beyond a $13.50 FPGA?**
A: The architecture is vendor-agnostic Verilog. On a mid-range Xilinx or Lattice FPGA (50K+ LUT), we target N=64+ parallel banks at >4 Gops/s. On an ASIC, the power and performance characteristics improve by 10-100x due to custom routing and lower capacitance. A Xilinx Zynq-7020 port has already been validated in synthesis (287 LUT, 471 FF — under 1% utilization).

**Q: What's the business model?**
A: ARM-style IP licensing. We license the RTL (hardware design) to chip designers who integrate it into their SoCs, collecting per-design license fees and per-unit royalties. ARM operates this model at $4B revenue and 97% gross margin.
