# ATOMiK Friday Pitch Audit

Last updated: 2026-05-27

Perspective: skeptical but interested pre-seed VC reviewing the Friday packet for narrative clarity, design quality, diligence readiness, and meeting-time fit.

## Executive Verdict

The ATOMiK package is materially stronger after the proof-safe messaging pass. The story now has a credible wedge: constrained systems waste resources moving, scanning, syncing, replaying, or rebuilding state, and ATOMiK evaluates whether meaningful-change tracking reduces that waste for a specific workload.

The biggest remaining risk is not overclaiming. That has mostly been controlled. The biggest remaining risk is presentation density. The deck and documents need to make the investor remember three things quickly:

1. ATOMiK makes change the unit of compute.
2. The next value gate is measured workload proof, not a broad compute promise.
3. The $2.0M ask funds customer workload evidence, IP diligence, ASIC feasibility, and licensing readiness.

## What A VC Wants To Receive

### Front-of-room assets

- A 10-minute deck that can be understood in five seconds per slide.
- A one-page PDF that survives forwarding without Matt or Allison in the room.
- A 2-page executive summary that states what is proven, what is not proven, and what the money buys.
- A crisp talk track with 5-minute, 10-minute, and 30-minute modes.
- A short proof appendix that does not bury the room in repo details.

### Follow-up diligence assets

- Claims and evidence matrix.
- Artifact index with public / controlled / NDA-only labels.
- Formula-backed financial workbook with monthly burn, runway by scenario, cash plan, evaluation-pricing treatment, and dilution sensitivity.
- Customer evaluation SOW template: workload, baseline, success metric, deliverables, fee treatment, confidentiality, IP, and claim boundaries.
- IP status memo: current filings, ownership, counsel gaps, conversion timing, and public-language rules.

## Content Audit

### Strong

- The buyer narrative starts with paid pain instead of architecture novelty.
- The ICP is narrow enough to qualify real design-partner conversations: one workload, one baseline, one painful constraint.
- Proof is evidence-labeled and caveated.
- The pitch does not depend on claiming battery gains, heat reduction, water savings, production readiness, or universal speedups.
- The ask is connected to measurable evidence gates rather than vague company-building spend.

### Needs More Work

- The canonical public workload should be selected and named. Best default: dirty-state telemetry sync / constrained state transfer.
- The strongest proof slide should show one clean artifact card, not every proof category at once.
- The business model needs a sharper near-term conversion path: proof review -> paid technical evaluation -> scoped design partner -> licensing/IP diligence.
- The financial workbook is now formula-backed, but still founder-prepared; final terms, vendor quotes, tax/accounting, and cap-table records require CFO/counsel review.
- Legal, IP, customer, and team materials are honest but thin. They need owner names, due dates, and status.

## Design And Aesthetic Audit

### Current Direction

The packet now has a coherent dark technical tone, proof-label discipline, and a premium deep-tech posture. It avoids the worst category errors: it is not mascot-led, not generic SaaS, and not claiming a finished commercial chip.

### Needed Enhancements

- Make slides more cinematic: one headline, one visual, one takeaway.
- Remove or reduce header metadata on slides where it competes with the message.
- Use larger type and fewer words. Dense proof language belongs in notes or appendix.
- Use proof labels as visual stamps, not paragraph labels.
- Keep Atom AI, if used, to cover/process/Q&A divider roles. Do not place it on benchmark, legal, financial, or caveat slides.
- Put the most important phrase on the slide as display copy: "Bring one workload. Measure the waste. Decide with evidence."
- Use a single repeated visual metaphor: full-state bulk path collapsing into delta path.

## Timing Audit

### 5-Minute Meeting

Use only:

1. Hook: ATOMiK makes change the unit of compute.
2. Pain: repeated state work creates battery, bandwidth, heat, latency, and reliability pressure.
3. Mechanism: reference state plus accumulated change.
4. Proof: hardware-backed path, Linux userspace-to-FPGA validation, AX7020 measured matrix with caveats.
5. Ask: $2.0M plus design-partner and workload introductions.

Do not use market sizing, detailed use cases, or full financial detail.

### 10-Minute Meeting

Default to a 9-minute talk and reserve 1 minute for interruption recovery. Proof should be 90 seconds, not 3 minutes. Market should be 45 seconds.

### 30-Minute Meeting

Pitch for 18 minutes, then Q&A for 12. Lead Q&A from proof boundaries, not speculative upside.

## Unsupported Claims To Keep Out

- Universal speedups.
- Guaranteed battery extension.
- Guaranteed heat reduction.
- Cooling or water savings.
- Guaranteed smaller hardware footprint.
- Production-ready silicon.
- CPU, GPU, NPU, compression, caching, or sync-protocol replacement.
- Signed customer traction, LOIs, or revenue unless signed evidence exists.

## Best Friday Close

ATOMiK is not asking the market to accept a broad compute claim. We are asking the right customers to bring one constrained state path, measure the waste, and decide with evidence whether state-aware compute belongs in their architecture.
